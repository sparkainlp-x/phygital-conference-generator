## 1. The idea

One conference, two engines. Engine 1 is holographic marketing for local
businesses; Engine 2 is a governed AI Community Centre where creators lead
the cultural design. The Spark AI Matrix is the platform
(doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23644437); WorldHoloBridge is the program
(doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23423941). Demos by appointment: people come, see
the display working with their own eyes, then decide.

## 3. Holographic technology, phase by phase

The hardware improves every year and falls in price. The durable layer is the
service around it. Spark builds no display hardware and claims no proprietary
display technology. Three holographies, kept distinct: optical wavefront
holography (research), membrane holographic memory (research models), and
LED fan display (what the conferences actually use).

### H1 - LED fan display

A strip of LEDs spins fast enough that persistence of vision blends the lit points into a floating image. Simple steps, in the right order, very fast, with the eyes doing the last step.

**Fits:** Every demo, every event. The workhorse.

**Honest limit:** Flat plane, not a volume. No depth, occlusion, or parallax. Needs a dark background; weak in bright daylight.

### H2 - Frame-art prototype

Battery-powered, wall-mountable frame showing holographic-style art and offers, with NFC/QR. In development.

**Fits:** Shops, hotel lobbies, visitor venues. Gift-card incentive and tourism interaction.

**Honest limit:** Concept today. No launch date, no price promised. A real merchant offer and a tested QR/NFC destination come first.

### H3 - Multi-fan wall

Fans tiled into a wall (up to a 12-fan wall).

**Fits:** Event-scale stages, atriums, flagship days.

**Honest limit:** Same flat-plane limit as H1, at larger scale. Safety review before every public session.

### H4 - Light-field display

True depth to several viewers at once, no glasses or headset. Looking Glass sells 16, 27, and 86-inch units; Sony ELF-MN1 (6-inch, eye tracking, ~US$343) released in Japan December 11, 2026.

**Fits:** Museum artefacts and premium pieces - objects too fragile to handle, shown in depth.

**Honest limit:** Higher price; the Sony unit serves one viewer with a narrow content library.

### H5 - Wavefront holography

Reconstructing light fields with spatial light modulators, rather than showing a flat image.

**Fits:** Research direction only.

**Honest limit:** Development-stage technology, not a commercial product. Spark claims nothing in this tier.

## 4. Worked examples for Canada

### 4.1 Museum - the Hall of Memory

One room, one supervised AI persona, a defined host agreement. An accessible
space where participants explore approved local history, ask questions, and
contribute stories through a consent-based process. Fragile objects are shown
on a light-field display (H4) in depth - seen, never handled. Everyday
objects are shown on the LED fan (H1). The first avatar in the pilot document
is Marie Curie; the figure is swappable per museum and per community, with
the community's approval. Flagship sectors here: tourism, museums, hotels, retail. Cultural
authorities: First Nations communities, Acadian communities, local cultural organizations.

### 4.2 Publicity - the Cadre artistique

A wall-mountable artistic frame with a holographic display and a QR code.
The frame carries the business's own art; the display carries the offer;
the QR code carries the promo and the gift card. A real merchant offer with
a real, tested QR/NFC destination - no invented discount, no fake QR.
Monthly programming keeps the offer fresh. Flagship sectors here: tourism, museums, hotels, retail.

## 5. AI governance - the twelve rules

1. **Consent first.** No story is shared without approval from the creators and communities authorized to grant it. Consent carries an expiry, a withdrawal path, and prohibited uses. Indigenous material only by a contributor flagged as an Indigenous governance authority, never for external AI training.
2. **Cultural authority approves every story.** The authority controls story approval, persona scope, content removal, and sponsor visibility within cultural stories.
3. **Sponsor firewall.** Sponsors may fund infrastructure, equipment, connectivity, translation, venue costs, or events. Sponsors may not approve, edit, veto, or control community stories.
4. **Human oversight.** AI-generated advertising and community content is reviewed by a person before public display. No fully automated public content.
5. **Honest avatars.** Every avatar is clearly labelled an AI representation, based on sourced material, correctable openly, and never presented as the real person. No avatars of living people without explicit consent.
6. **Privacy by design.** Minimum personal information collected. Engagement measured in aggregate, not by surveillance or visitor tracking. Avatars do not retain personal conversations. Session memory is cleared at the end of every visit.
7. **Honest claims.** No verified lower bound, no headline. Negative findings, denominators, and uncertainty are reported, not hidden.
8. **Audit ledger.** Every approval, change, incident, and firewall check is recorded in an append-only log.
9. **Guardrails cannot be disabled.** A property of the reference prototype; a governed deployment keeps it that way.
10. **Accessibility is a gate, not a feature.** Four accessibility features required before publication. Target: WCAG 2.2 AA. Disability-led, with people with disabilities on the team, not as an afterthought.
11. **Defensive, human-gated, non-lethal.** The mission is AI for humanity. No autonomous lethal use, no targeting. Ever.
12. **Open by default.** The prototype and the generator are open code. What is sold is deployment, customization, training, and the conference - not secrecy.
