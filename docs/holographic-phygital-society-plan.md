## 1. The idea in one page

**A phygital society is a society where physical presence and digital content meet in the open, in the same room, with the same rules.** A product floats in light where people can see it with their own eyes. A story is told by the community that owns it, in the language they choose, with their approval on record. Nobody has to wear a headset, download an app, or surrender their data to take part.

**The conference is the delivery vehicle.** WorldHoloBridge is one conference with two engines:

- **Engine 1 — Holographic marketing.** Demos, installations, and event activations that bring customers through the door for local businesses — hotels, tourism attractions, museums, shops.
- **Engine 2 — A governed AI Community Centre.** The same tools, used by the community itself: creators lead the cultural design, young people learn, stories are told with consent and on the record.

**The Spark AI Matrix is the platform; WorldHoloBridge is the program.** The Matrix is the safe-AI learning framework, published openly on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23644437 — the most-read 2026 record: 178 views, 81 downloads). WorldHoloBridge is the conference and demonstration program through which the Matrix is delivered, with its governance reference prototype published openly on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23423941).

**The plan adapts to any country.** The core never changes — the phases, the governance rules, the honest technology description, the demo tiers. What changes is the country profile: languages, cultural authorities, flagship sectors, venues, funding bodies, disability organizations, and the legal framework for consent. A Python generator (Section 10) produces the per-country plan from that profile, so every new country starts from the same verified core instead of a blank page.

**The proof model never changes either: demos by appointment.** People come, see the display working with their own eyes, then decide. The proof travels in the demo kit instead of relying on description. Equipment stays Spark AI NLP's under demo arrangements, and demos are operated by Spark-trained operators only.

## 2. The conference, phase by phase

### Phase 0 — Country profile

**What happens:** Language(s), cultural authorities, flagship sectors, venues, funding body, disability organizations, consent law. Written down, not assumed.

**Gate:** Profile complete and reviewed by a local contact.

### Phase 1 — Discovery & consent

**What happens:** Map the cultural authority for every story and persona. Set the sponsor firewall. Name the accessibility lead, the technical lead, and the privacy lead.

**Gate:** Cultural authority named; firewall agreed in writing.

### Phase 2 — Pilot demos

**What happens:** Demos by appointment (Demo 1/2/3). One museum pilot and one publicity pilot, each small, each measured.

**Gate:** Paid demand tested; delivery costs recorded.

### Phase 3 — Flagship conference

**What happens:** One day. Public educational session (60–75 minutes) plus 4–6 private briefings of 20–30 minutes for pre-qualified leads. Five zones (Section 2.1).

**Gate:** Targets set: 75–125 attendees, 4–6 briefings, 25+ leads, 3+ proposals, 1–2 paid pilots in 6 months.

### Phase 4 — Community Centre pilot

**What happens:** 90-day Country Node pilot (Section 2.2). One room, one supervised AI persona, a defined host agreement. Evidence Passport at the end.

**Gate:** Passport complete; stop rules respected.

### Phase 5 — National node & region

**What happens:** Repeat what worked. Establish service capacity. Only then consider the next country or region — stages, never a blitz.

**Gate:** Atlantic contracts closing (Canada) or the equivalent evidence in that country.

### 2.1 The flagship day — five zones

1. **Welcome & proof.** The display working, in the open, with a person operating it. Visitors see it first, before any pitch.
2. **Business demos.** The Demo tiers running (Section 8). Pre-qualified briefings by appointment.
3. **Museum corner.** A Hall of Memory station and a light-field artefact display (Section 4.1).
4. **Publicity corner.** A Cadre artistique frame with a live QR/NFC offer (Section 4.2).
5. **Governance corner.** The consent register, the audit log, and the Evidence Passport on display — governance you can point at, not a promise you have to take on trust.

### 2.2 The 90-day Country Node pilot

- **Days 1–14, Preparation.** Approvals: cultural authority, accessibility lead, technical lead, privacy, governance firewall, AI persona lease, display and fan safety review. Evidence Passport created.
- **Days 15–30, Soft launch.** Controlled opening, mediator-led sessions, issues logged.
- **Days 31–75, Public pilot.** Public operation; participation and engagement measured in aggregate.
- **Days 76–90, Review.** Completed Country Node Evidence Passport: participation, accessibility, safety, governance, technical findings, lessons learned.
- **Stop rules.** Pause or stop for cultural-authority withdrawal, a serious accessibility, privacy, or safety failure, repeated harmful AI output, a governance breach, or an unresolved hardware risk.

### 2.3 Expansion, in stages, never a blitz

1. Demonstrate delivery and paid demand in the first city.
2. Repeat successful applications and establish service capacity.
3. Consider the next cities in the same country.
4. Assess the next country individually, only once contracts are closing in the current one.

Expansion depends on customer economics, operational capacity, and signed agreements — not a predetermined touring schedule.

## 3. Holographic technology, phase by phase

The hardware improves every year and falls in price. The durable layer is the service around it. The pilot validates the service — local installation, bilingual content, governance, audience trust — not one hardware generation. When better hardware reaches Spark's price point, it slots into the same service model.

### H1 — LED fan display

**What it is:** A strip of LEDs spins fast enough that persistence of vision blends the lit points into a floating image. Simple steps, in the right order, very fast, with the eyes doing the last step.

**Where it fits:** Every demo, every event. The workhorse.

**Honest limit:** Flat plane, not a volume. No depth, occlusion, or parallax. Needs a dark background; weak in bright daylight.

### H2 — Frame-art prototype

**What it is:** Battery-powered, wall-mountable frame showing holographic-style art and offers, with NFC/QR. In development.

**Where it fits:** Shops, hotel lobbies, visitor venues. Gift-card incentive and tourism interaction.

**Honest limit:** Concept today. No launch date, no price promised. A real merchant offer and a tested QR/NFC destination come first.

### H3 — Multi-fan wall

**What it is:** Fans tiled into a wall (up to a 12-fan wall).

**Where it fits:** Event-scale stages, atriums, flagship days.

**Honest limit:** Same flat-plane limit as H1, at larger scale. Safety review before every public session.

### H4 — Light-field display

**What it is:** True depth to several viewers at once, no glasses or headset. Looking Glass sells 16, 27, and 86-inch units; Sony ELF-MN1 (6-inch, eye tracking, ~US$343) released in Japan December 11, 2026.

**Where it fits:** Museum artefacts and premium pieces — objects too fragile to handle, shown in depth.

**Honest limit:** Higher price; the Sony unit serves one viewer with a narrow content library.

### H5 — Wavefront holography

**What it is:** Reconstructing light fields with spatial light modulators, rather than showing a flat image.

**Where it fits:** Research direction only.

**Honest limit:** Development-stage technology, not a commercial product. Spark claims nothing in this tier.

**Three holographies, kept distinct — never blurred in public copy:**

1. **Optical wavefront holography** — reconstructing light fields (H5, research).
2. **Membrane holographic memory** — the research models (membrane-arena, tensor-membrane), synthetic and classical.
3. **LED fan display** — what the conferences actually use (H1–H3).

Spark builds no display hardware and claims no proprietary display technology. The differentiation is the integration: display equipment, bilingual content, accessible delivery, local service, and community programming.

**Typical LED fan specifications** (ranges across current vendor listings; confirm per unit before quoting a client): 42 cm desktop (~450×224 to 1024×384, ~800 nits, 15–20 W); 65 cm (~1024×1024 to 1600×960, ~800–2,600 nits, 36–60 W); 100 cm (~1600×1024 to 2048×1440, ~800–2,600 nits, 50–150 W); up to 280 cm outdoor (IP65, up to ~4,000 nits). Viewing angle 150–176°. Rotation roughly 650–1,300 rpm. Content is MP4 video with a pure black background at the unit's native resolution; black in the content is transparent.

**Safety, every time.** Guarded, secured fan mounts; brightness, distance, and duration guidance posted at each station; flicker assessment completed before every public session.

## 4. Two worked examples

### 4.1 Museum — the Hall of Memory

A museum is the cleanest first pilot for the community engine, because a museum already knows what it means to hold objects in trust.

- **The room.** One room, one supervised AI persona, a defined host agreement. The Hall of Memory is an accessible space where participants explore approved local history, ask questions, and contribute stories through a consent-based process. Before opening, the pilot establishes who operates and pays for it, who owns the equipment, who approves stories and persona use, who handles privacy and incident response, how participants request corrections or withdraw permission, and what evidence is needed before expanding.
- **The persona.** One avatar, clearly labelled an AI representation, based on sourced material, correctable openly, never presented as the real person. The first avatar in the pilot document is Marie Curie; the figure is swappable per museum and per community, with the community's approval. No avatars of living people without explicit consent.
- **The artefacts.** Fragile objects the museum cannot put on open display are shown on a light-field display (H4) in depth — the object is seen, never handled. Everyday objects are shown on the LED fan (H1) in the gallery.
- **The kiosk.** A bilingual (local languages) station where visitors tap or scan for a story, an offer, or a local experience. Every story on the kiosk carries its approval on the audit log.
- **Access.** Seated viewing, captions, transcripts, audio description, sign-language interpretation by human interpreters on request, reduced-motion mode, quiet hours, step-free throughout. Target: WCAG 2.2 AA.
- **What the museum measures.** Visitors who stop, visitors who ask a question, stories contributed with consent, corrections requested and made, accessibility issues logged and resolved. Attendance alone is not presented as success.

### 4.2 Publicity — the Cadre artistique

A shop, a hotel, or a tourism attraction is the cleanest first pilot for the commercial engine, because the result is a customer walking through the door.

- **The frame.** A wall-mountable artistic frame with a holographic display and a QR code (H2). The frame carries the business's own art; the display carries the offer; the QR code carries the promo and the gift card.
- **The offer.** A real merchant offer with a real, tested QR/NFC destination. No invented discount, no fake QR, no mirrored text. The gift-card incentive is a concept until a real merchant offer and a tested destination exist.
- **The loop.** The display is bright-on-black, designed for the fan's flat plane. The customer sees the product floating in light, taps or scans, and the offer lands on their phone. The business sees the scans, the redemptions, and the foot traffic — measured, not asserted. No "5x attention" or "+30% foot traffic" claims without a measured source.
- **The programming.** Monthly content updates ($1,500/month, 3–6 month minimum): the offer changes with the season, the playlist refreshes, the device is managed remotely. The business buys a service, not a piece of equipment.
- **What the business measures.** Scans, redemptions, and repeat visits. A lack of sales is reported and used to revise the offer — not presented as success through attendance figures alone.

## 7. AI governance — the rules

These rules come from the ACOA core and the WorldHoloBridge prototype. They do not change between countries; only the names of the authorities and the local law change.

1. **Consent first.** No story is shared without approval from the creators and communities authorized to grant it. Consent carries an expiry, a withdrawal path, and prohibited uses. Indigenous material only by a contributor flagged as an Indigenous governance authority, never for external AI training.
2. **Cultural authority approves every story.** The authority controls story approval, persona scope, content removal, and sponsor visibility within cultural stories.
3. **Sponsor firewall.** Sponsors may fund infrastructure, equipment, connectivity, translation, venue costs, or events. Sponsors may not approve, edit, veto, or control community stories.
4. **Human oversight.** AI-generated advertising and community content is reviewed by a person before public display. No fully automated public content.
5. **Honest avatars.** Every avatar is clearly labelled an AI representation, based on sourced material, correctable openly, and never presented as the real person. No avatars of living people without explicit consent.
6. **Privacy by design.** Minimum personal information collected. Engagement measured in aggregate, not by surveillance or visitor tracking. Avatars do not retain personal conversations. Session memory is cleared at the end of every visit.
7. **Honest claims.** No verified lower bound, no headline. Negative findings, denominators, and uncertainty are reported, not hidden.
8. **Audit ledger.** Every approval, change, incident, and firewall check is recorded in an append-only log.
9. **Guardrails cannot be disabled.** A property of the reference prototype; a governed deployment keeps it that way.
10. **Accessibility is a gate, not a feature.** Four accessibility features required before publication. Target: WCAG 2.2 AA. Disability-led, with people with disabilities on the team, not as an afterthought.
11. **Defensive, human-gated, non-lethal.** The mission is AI for humanity. No autonomous lethal use, no targeting. Ever.
12. **Open by default.** The prototype and the generator are open code. What is sold is deployment, customization, training, and the conference — not secrecy.

## 10. The generator — one core, any country

The Python generator (`phygital-conference-generator`, Section 6.1) produces a per-country conference plan from a country profile. The ACOA core is the fixed content; the profile is the variable content.

**A country profile** (JSON) carries: country, languages, cultural authorities, flagship sectors, venues, funding body, disability organizations, consent-law note, and a currency note. Five profiles ship with it: Canada, Morocco, Germany, France, UK.

**What it writes:** a Markdown conference plan for that country — the phases (Section 2), the holographic tiers (Section 3), the museum and publicity examples adapted to that country's sectors (Section 4), the governance checklist (Section 7), the budget bands (Section 8), and the 90-day pilot (Section 2.2).

**How to run it:**

```
python -m phygital_conference --country Morocco --output plans/morocco-conference-plan.md
python -m phygital_conference --country Canada --output plans/canada-conference-plan.md
python -m phygital_conference --list-countries
```

**How to add a country:** copy `profiles/canada.json`, fill in the fields, and run the generator. No code change. A new profile is validated before anything is written: every required field must be present, and the governance checklist is always emitted in full — a profile cannot silently drop a rule.

**Tests:** the package ships with a pytest suite that checks the profile validation, that every generated plan contains all six phases, all five holographic tiers, both worked examples, the full twelve-rule governance checklist, and the demo tiers with the adopted prices. A plan that fails any of those checks is not written.
